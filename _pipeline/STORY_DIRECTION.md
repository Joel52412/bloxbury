# Bloxbury story & scare direction playbook
Studio-grade first drafts (Joel). Short rules are in CLAUDE.md; this is the reasoning and method.

## Research findings (22 Nov) and what we do with them
- **Completion decides reach.** TikTok ranks by watch completion; reported viral bar has risen from ~50% (2024) to ~70% (2026).
  How many viewers stay past the 3rd second predicts most of the final view count.
  -> Picture AND line hook together in 0-3 s. No slow intros, no title cards.
- **Open loops, stacked.** One question holds a viewer a few seconds; several stacked questions hold them to the end.
  -> Every ~8-10 s answer one question and open a new one before the old one closes.
- **Pattern breaks** (3 normal beats, then 1 wrong one) are among the best-retaining structures.
- **Familiar made wrong** (Joel's "nostalgia" theory, confirmed): analog horror / Skinamarink / Backrooms went huge on TikTok by
  twisting familiar homes, people and media. Retelling a story people half-know (urban legends, viral creepypastas) in the
  Roblox format gives instant recognition plus a new twist.
- **Dread > loud stings.** Brain studies separate slow dread from the acute jump-scare startle; the best horror alternates them.
  A good startle is misdirection ("a magic trick"), not volume.
- **Rewatches are a strong signal.** -> Hide details that reward a second watch, and make the last line flow into the first.

## Method for writing a part
1. Start from the previous part's cliffhanger and **reframe** something the audience thought they understood (payoff for returners).
2. Pick a half-known legend/story to retell (Part 2: the viral "Mum calls you to the kitchen... the real Mum whispers
   'I heard it too'" story).
3. Beat sheet with timestamps: hook (0-3 s), pattern break, then a new open loop every 8-10 s, one earned scare, a quiet final line.
4. Scare toolkit (pick, don't repeat between parts): silence / music dropout, misdirection, delayed reflection, the wrong voice
   from the wrong place, a detail in plain sight, a nickname or word that lands like a hit. At most ONE real startle per part.
5. Rewatch bait: 1-2 hidden details (e.g. a 4-frame reflection). End on a comment-choice question. Last line -> first line loop.
6. Continuity: NOT in the first 3 s. Part 2 opened on a dark recap + ~0.75 s of black and lost scrollers. Hook new viewers first
   (lit picture, voice by 0.3 s, impossible claim, e.g. viral plane video: "I've been on this plane for almost thirty hours"),
   then bridge to the previous part around 3-10 s.
7. Ad-safe: creepy not gory, nothing graphic in the first 7 s or the thumbnail.
